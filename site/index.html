What It Does

Catches A Post Under A DoorPut a walk door where a post lands and it tells you in plain words, then offers to slide the door clear.→ Prices From Your Own ListLoad a spreadsheet of your supplier's prices. Add your markup, labor and sales tax, and the total keeps up.→ Prints The PaperworkA proposal for the customer, a cut list for the crew and framing drawings of every wall. The material order saves as a spreadsheet for the yard.→ Sends The Customer A LinkThey open it on a phone and turn their own building around. Your costs and markup stay with you.→ Works On A Tablet Without SignalOpen it once and it keeps working in a field with no bars. The job saves on the tablet.→ Draws More Than A Plain BoxGable, single slope and gambrel roofs. Lean-tos and porches on any wall, open walls, lofts, interior walls, cupolas, skylights and eave lights.→ Checks The Lot And The TractorType in the lot and the setbacks and it draws the site plan. Type in the size of a tractor or camper and it tells you which door it fits through.→ Closes The SaleOffer good, better and best side by side, set the payment schedule, and let the customer sign on the screen.→ Packs A Folder For Your EngineerIt does not do the engineering. It hands your engineer the size, the framing as drawn, every post and opening, and the drawings.→

No Charge For Shops Of Ten Or Fewer

Every company gets 30 days with the whole program. After that it asks for a license code. Builders and yards with ten people or fewer get theirs without paying, and larger companies buy a license. The code is public for anyone to read, which makes it source-available.

Where It Stands Today

It draws gable, single slope and gambrel buildings up to 100 feet wide, with lean-tos, porches, lofts, interior walls, liner and insulation. Its framing rules are still being checked against published builder guides, and the drawings say so until that is done.
