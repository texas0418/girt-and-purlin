What It Does

Everything on this page was made by the program from one sample building: a 30 by 48 machine shed with 12 foot walls, a sliding door, a walk door and two windows.

Lay Out The Building

Set the width, length, wall height, roof pitch and overhang with arrow buttons. Pick the post spacing and truss spacing, for the whole building or wall by wall. Click a wall to put a sliding door, overhead door, walk door or window on it, then nudge it along the wall.

The building redraws in 3D as you go, in your colors. Switch to Framing to see the posts, girts, trusses and purlins with the steel off, or to Floor Plan for the view from above.

Three Roof ShapesGable, single slope and gambrel. The side overhang and the end overhang are set apart from each other.
Lean-Tos And PorchesOn any wall, open or closed in, each with its own depth, pitch and drop below the main eave.

Inside The BuildingInterior walls with doors, a loft at either end, steel liner on the walls and ceiling, and insulation for the roof, the walls and the ceiling.
Light And TrimClear skylight panels in the roof, a 3 foot strip of clear eave light panels at the top of a side wall, wainscot, cupolas and gutters.
In The GroundPosts set in the ground or on brackets, with footings, concrete and a slab counted in the material list.

Catches A Post Under A Door

Posts go in at your spacing. A door wide enough to need them gets a post at each side. When a walk door or window lands on a post, the program stops you with a line like the one here and a Fix It For Me button that slides the opening clear.

Check this. The walk door on the front wall lands on the post at 8 ft 0 in. Slide it 1 ft 6 in to clear the post, or move the post.

Prices From Your Own List

The material list counts posts by length, trusses, skirt boards, girts, purlins, headers, roof and wall panels, trim, screws, concrete, gutters and everything you add inside. It updates on every change and marks the lines that moved.

A price list is a spreadsheet saved as CSV with four columns: Item, Size, Unit and Price. Load yours, or several from different suppliers, set your markup, your labor per square foot and your sales tax, and the total follows. The program ships with sample prices so the screen has something to add up. They are not real quotes.

Checks The Lot And The Tractor

Site PlanType in the lot width and depth, where the building sits and the setbacks your permit office gave you. It draws the lot with the distance to every line and says so when the building crosses a setback.
Test FitType in the length, width and height of a tractor, camper or boat from its spec sheet. It tells you which doors it goes through, how much room is to spare, and whether it clears the loft and the interior walls.
Site ConditionsRecord the snow load, wind speed, exposure and frost depth the permit office gave you. They print on the engineer package, and a frost depth past 4 feet sets the posts deeper.

Closes The Sale

Good, Better, BestKeep up to four versions of one job and print them side by side on the proposal, each with its own price.
Payment Schedule And SignatureSet the deposit and the payments. The customer signs on the screen with a finger or a mouse and the signature prints on the proposal.
Templates And Saved JobsKeep the buildings you sell often as templates and start each new job from one.
A Designer For Your Own WebsitePut a simple version of the designer on your site. A customer lays out a building and sends it to you. They never see your costs.
For Your Books And Your CADThe job saves as an invoice file for QuickBooks and the drawings save as DXF for a CAD program.
Delivery Check SheetA printed list of every item on the order with a box to tick as it comes off the truck.

Sends The Customer A Link

Send To Customer makes a link you paste into a text or an email. The customer opens a look-only page with their building in 3D, the floor plan, what is included and the total. Your costs, your markup and the material list are not in it. The picture at the top of the front page is that same view.

Packs A Folder For Your Engineer

The program does not size anything for load. It counts footings and a slab as material only, and it has no bracing, no truss design and no connection details. What it does is save your engineer the first hour: a package with the building's size, the framing as drawn with room to mark changes, a table of every post and every opening, the site conditions you typed in, the post layout and the framing drawings, plus a data file they can load into their own software.

Not In It Yet

  • Hip roofs, dormers, awnings and porches that wrap a corner.
  • Buildings that need a post longer than 24 feet. Widths run to 100 feet, but a wall near 24 feet tall is turned away with a reason.
  • Engineering of any kind. The snow, wind and frost figures you type in are recorded and passed to your engineer. Nothing is sized from them except post depth for deep frost.
  • Truss types. Every truss is counted as one ordered item at the span and pitch, with no choice of scissor, attic or others.
  • Coverings other than ribbed steel panels. No shingles, standing seam, lap siding or board and batten.
  • Stud-framed walls and buildings joined to one another.
  • Eave lights on the printed framing drawings. They show in the 3D picture and the material list.
  • Panel counts that subtract door and window openings. Today the walls are counted solid.
  • Excel files. Price lists load as CSV.
  • Typing a number straight into a size box. Sizes move by arrow buttons.
